# Lawful sourcing: what you may put through a client

BitTorrent judges nothing; the law judges the content. This page lists the
categories that are lawful to download and share, so legitimate users know
they are the audience this software is built for. It is not a directory —
no indexers, no groups, no sources are named here, for anything.

> **Not legal advice.** General information for orientation only.

## 1. Free and open-source software

Linux distributions, BSDs, LibreOffice, and countless tools ship official
torrents. Checksummed, resumable, and faster than mirrors at release time.
The canonical lawful torrent use — if this is you, welcome home.

## 2. Public-domain film and texts

Copyright expires. In the US, works published before 1929 are broadly
public domain, as are famous one-offs whose copyright was never secured
(*Night of the Living Dead*, 1968). The Internet Archive hosts thousands
of such films lawfully and freely: silent-era classics, early noir,
ephemeral and educational films. Project Gutenberg does the same for books
(this repo already searches it directly). Few titles compared to commercial
catalogs, but real films, lawful to download, keep, and screen.

## 3. Creator-released works

The Blender Foundation open movies (*Big Buck Bunny*, *Sintel*,
*Tears of Steel*), Creative-Commons music archives, game studios' official
torrent patches (historical Blizzard practice), and artists who release via
torrent deliberately. The creator's permission is what makes these lawful —
look for it explicitly.

## 4. Your own files

Moving your own large files between your own machines — a private swarm of
two peers (NAS and desktop), or a personal backup you can re-fetch after a
drive failure. No rights-holder exists to object, because it is you.

## 5. Open datasets

Academic, scientific, and government open-data projects distribute
terabyte-scale datasets by torrent because HTTP hosting would cost a
fortune. The license (usually CC0/CC-BY or equivalent) travels with the
data — keep it with the files.

## 6. What is NOT on this list

Commercial films, series, anime, manga, books, games, or software you have
not licensed — regardless of whether you "own it on another format,"
"can't buy it locally," "only download what you later buy," or "delete
after watching." None of those are legal doctrines anywhere in the
jurisdictions covered in [country specifics](LEGAL_COUNTRIES.md). And
uploading/seeding infringing content is distribution, treated more
severely than downloading nearly everywhere — torrent clients upload by
default while downloading, which is exactly why "I only downloaded" rarely
describes what actually happened. See [safety](SAFETY_TORRENTING.md) for
the technical side of that.
